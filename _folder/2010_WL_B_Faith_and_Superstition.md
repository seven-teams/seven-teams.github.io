<p style="text-align:center;">
One person’s faith is another person’s superstition<br>
This is as true for the faith in the rational<br>
As it is true for the faith in the supernatural<br>
<br>
So what is the difference of a healthy faith and superstition?<br>
<br>
Healthy faith is like a scientific hypothesis<br>
It develops and evolves, adapts and changes<br>
Constantly challenged and trained by new experiences<br>
<br>
Allowing understanding to gradually increase<br>
by learning and by listening<br>
without and within ourselves<br>
<br>
Faith can become superstition<br>
When it gets rigid and stale<br>
habitual and resistant to change<br>
<br>
Faith has become superstition<br>
when we forget that we have much to grow<br>
<br>
When others become wrong and we are always right<br>
When fear has replaced the will to listen and to learn<br>
<br>
The scientific method can help us with our faith<br>
If we stay aware that results<br>
should not be confused with facts<br>
Like experiences, they are just pieces<br>
in support or challenge to our faith<br>
<br>
Faith is needed<br>
when information and experiences are not enough<br>
to completely prove or disprove of our hypotheses<br>
<br>
Science itself is quite limited when it comes to truth<br>
Experiments give results when controlled and reproduced<br>
<br>
Occurrences in nature, though<br>
are seldom controlled and mostly quite unique<br>
<br>
The supernatural, if it exists<br>
would be resistant to control and difficult to reproduce<br>
<br>
Single events are easily dismissed<br>
if they don’t fit a pattern, outliers so to say<br>
<br>
Something would be supernatural<br>
when it’s beyond our control and current understanding<br>
<br>
Its existence open to our faith<br>
open to experiences, open to interpretation<br>
<br>
Where does this leave us in our desire for the rational<br>
desire for the truth?<br>
<br>
It was said that love is truth and truth is love<br>
Love to give and love to get<br>
Truth and love result from understanding<br>
Often far beyond the simply rational
</p>
